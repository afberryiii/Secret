The cold, digital threat of the text message is a secret you swallow whole, the shock too intense to share right now. "Uh, look at that," you stammer, trying to sound casual, "I just got a notification about the school paper—they posted the prom photos. Nothing important. A slow tension seems to ease out of her shoulders, though her eyes remain wary. "Good," she replies, her voice tight. "Let's just get to class. We need to focus on this Chem test and forget about last night, all right?" You nod, forcing yourself to walk beside her as if everything is fine. But every casual sound—the squeak of sneakers on the floor, the slam of a locker door—jumps at you. The knowledge that someone is watching, someone who knows you're in class, turns the familiar hallway into a hostile, crowded maze. The text is a live wire burning in your pocket, and you are terrified to share it, knowing it will shatter the illusion of safety, but terrified to keep it, knowing it might be the only clue.
