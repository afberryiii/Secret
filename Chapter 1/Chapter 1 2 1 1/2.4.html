"I know it sounds crazy, but that dream... it felt so real, Chloe," you say, lowering your voice as you walk down the hall. You stop by her locker, leaning against the cold metal. "When I woke up, I was honestly checking my side for a stab wound. It was Ghostface, and he was chasing us right after we left prom, down your street." Chloe closes her locker slowly, her smile completely gone. Her eyes scan the crowded hallway nervously. "Listen, I told you it was just a nightmare. You've been binge-watching too much horror stuff, maybe? It happens." "No, this was different," you insist, your voice desperate for her to understand. "We were running, and I yelled for help, and we tried to hide in a car... and he was right there, tapping on the glass with the knife. It was terrifying. You were right beside me the whole time." She sighs, a sound of strained impatience. She leans in closer, her voice dropping to a near-whisper. "I was beside you on the walk home. I wasn't beside you when you were sleeping. It was a dream, okay? Let it go. We have a test in first period, and I don't want to think about fake murders right now." Her reaction is cold, dismissive, and it stings. But what worries you more is the look in her eyes—it's not just annoyance; it's something guarded, almost secretive. She avoids your gaze and then quickly changes the subject, pulling you toward the classroom. "Come on, we're going to be late. Did you study for Mr. Harrison's Chem test? Because I totally bombed the practice quiz." The immediate, pressing reality of school pushes the dream back, but her strange defensiveness leaves a chilling residue.
